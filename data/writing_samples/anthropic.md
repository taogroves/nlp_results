For much of my academic career, I believed I was an engineer at heart. I was drawn to solving concrete problems and and building products that worked reliably at scale. Through my research experiences, however, I have come to realize that what excites me most is not just building systems using existing technologies, but discovering what is possible. I am most energized when I can take a new idea, iterate quickly, run experiments, and turn an abstract hypothesis into something real.

My work spans several fields across CS and ML; I have worked with large-scale machine learning workloads, including benchmarking GPUs and analyzing scaling tradeoffs in transformer architectures. I have also contributed to multimodal research, developing diffusion, vision, and transfomer models and releasing large datasets. These experiences have shaped my preference for industrial, “big science” research over academia. I want to be directly involved in building the next generation of AI systems, where advances in architecture, scale, and infrastructure translate immediately into real-world impact, and I get to have a say in how my work is applied.
